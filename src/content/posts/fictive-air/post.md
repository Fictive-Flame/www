At Fictive Flame, storytelling has always been at the heart of everything we do. Over the last year, that mission led us into the skies. Earlier this year, when our Founder and Creative Director earned his Part 107 certificate, we joined the ranks of professional drone operators in Colorado.

Becoming FAA Certified was one of our goals this year, and it's not just performative: it protects us, and you, as the client, from large fines and allows us to fly with confidence in airspace that would otherwise be off limits to recreational pilots.

Today, we're entering a new chapter of professional aerial imaging, as we move from being a certified and insured drone operator, to a builder of custom aircraft. Introducing Fictive Air, an American-made, Category 1 multirotor drone, designed for high-end cinematic flying in close proximity to people and vehicles. It's built for advanced pilots, tuned for precision, and compliant at liftoff.

# Why We Built It

FPV, or "First Person View" drones set the standard for immersive, cinematic shots. It's an experience like no other, allowing a skilled pilot to loop, dive, and speed though tiny gaps with precision and ease.

If what you crave is buttery smooth, stable footage in extremely tight spaces (and even flying inches from your subject) then there's only one style of build that will satisfy your needs: the cinewhoop.

Built from the DNA of often much smaller, acrobatic racing drones, cinewhoops use 2-3" props and guards to prevent skin laceration in the event of a crash. They're also lighter than most other drones, weighing roughly the same as a typical remote control. These features are the primary qualifiers for Category 1.

We already had a traditional camera drone, a Mavic 3 Pro, in our fleet, and after a week of experientation, concluded it wouldn't be able to fly where we needed it to&mdash;especially for summer events. The team needed something different: a drone that was under the weight limits, safe to operate over crowds, and close to moving vehicles. The one problem? We had a prebuilt cinewhoop on order, but it hasn't moved in almost a month.

Rather than compromise, we decided to build our own. Not just a drone, but a creative tool designed from the ground up to capture and share immersive stories.

# Category 1, Fully Compliant

One of the first requirements was to keep the build under 250g to meet FAA Category 1 requirements. This weight class allows flight over people without a waiver, which unlocks huge creative potential in urban environments, live events, and dynamic location shots.

Meeting this goal, while still hitting cinematic standards was the hard part. Every inch of wire, connector, and screw adds to the weight.

Another input to the process was the need to fly both indoors and out. One of the most satisfying drone shots is flying up to, and ultimately though, a building. This type of shot has been popularized by real estate tours, but it's a great effect nonetheless.

With finely tuned parts list, we hit the store:

- Frame that can accomodate 2.5" props with guards
- 1404 4600 KV motors
- 2.5" tri-blade HQ props
- Flywoo Goku GN745 AIO flight controller
- DJI O4 Pro Air Unit

While many builds of this type use 2" props, we opted for the extra .5" to mitigate performance loss and wind resistance.

# Designed for the Set, Not the Shelf

Fictive Air is a lightweight quadcopter with pro-grade stability, native 4K optics, and modular flexibility:

Under 250 grams – Fly over people legally, with no need for remote ID modules or additional FAA paperwork.
Native 4K camera system – Tuned for cinematic color science, with manual controls and flat profiles for post.
Carbon-fiber and composite frame – Rigid, lightweight, and built for real production environments.
Zero-latency digital video transmission – For precise control in tight, dynamic shots.
American-sourced electronics and final assembly – Reliable, secure, and compliant with domestic production standards.
This isn’t a drone repurposed for filmmakers—it’s a filmmaking tool that happens to fly.

Field-Tested by Filmmakers
Every decision—down to the motor size, prop pitch, and camera mounting angle—was guided by real-world production needs. We flew prototypes on rooftops, in crowded streets, through concert venues, and inside architectural interiors. We stress-tested the control links, fine-tuned stabilization in wind, and rebuilt every component that didn’t meet our standards.

The footage from Fictive Air has already made its way into music videos, commercials, and short films. Our operators have flown it legally in places where heavier drones would have been grounded.

The result is cinematic footage, captured with agility—and legally cleared for more scenarios than ever before.

The Bigger Picture
With Fictive Air, we're not just launching a drone—we’re reclaiming creative control. For too long, drone innovation has been led by companies far removed from the needs of production crews. We're changing that.

This drone was designed by the people who use it. Built domestically. Tuned for cinema. And most importantly, it’s ready to fly—safely and legally—without the regulatory burden that usually comes with professional aerial gear.
